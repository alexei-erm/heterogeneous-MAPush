Both runs produced nearly identical results — Anymal does all work, Go1 freeloads. The gating mechanism had no visible effect on behavior.

## Bug Found: Inverted Mass Normalization (CRITICAL)

### Root Cause

In `_compute_dual_push_balance()`, the mass normalization formula was **backwards**:

```python
# WRONG (was in both wrappers):
mass_fraction = self.cf_agent_masses[i] / M_total
contributions[:, i] = push_force_i * mass_fraction
```

This **amplifies** the heavier robot's contribution instead of normalizing it:

| Agent | Mass | Mass Fraction | Equal force F → contribution |
|-------|------|---------------|------------------------------|
| Go1 (12kg) | 12 | 0.19 | F × 0.19 |
| Anymal (50kg) | 50 | 0.81 | F × 0.81 |

Even with **identical** raw pushing force, balance = 0.19/0.81 = **0.24**. The gate could never approach 1.0 in a hetero setup, so `cf_gate` was a near-constant ~0.47 regardless of actual behavior — equivalent to just rescaling rewards uniformly. The policy adapts to a constant scale factor trivially, producing zero behavioral signal.

### Additional Issue: Threshold in Wrong Units

The 5.0N threshold was compared against mass-weighted contributions. With the inverted formula:
- Anymal needed ~6.2N raw force to trigger (easy for a 50kg robot)
- Go1 needed ~25.8N raw force to be max contributor (impossible for 12kg robot)

The threshold was effectively keyed only off Anymal's contribution, and when it fired, the balance was perpetually low due to the inverted normalization.

## Fix Applied (2 files)

### 1. Mass normalization: divide by mass instead of multiply

```python
# CORRECT (force-per-kg):
contributions[:, i] = push_force_i / self.cf_agent_masses[i]
```

Now equal physical effort → equal contributions → balance ≈ 1.0. The heavier robot's inherent force advantage is divided out.

### 2. Threshold adjusted: 5.0 N → 0.1 N/kg

Old threshold in new units would require 60N from Go1 — impossible. 0.1 N/kg = ~1.2N raw force from Go1 or ~5N from Anymal to trigger gating.

### 3. Removed unused `M_total` variable, updated print strings to show "N/kg"

## Key Insight

`contribution = force * mass_fraction` amplifies the heavier robot. `contribution = force / mass` normalizes it. The difference: with equal raw force, old formula gives balance=0.24 (constant penalty), new formula gives balance=1.0 (no penalty). The gate now correctly distinguishes balanced from unbalanced pushing.
